## The Gate Architecture

Every candidate signal—a document, a filing, a policy statement, a reported fact—passes through four deterministic gates in sequence. All four must pass. There is no override, no editorial exception, no "close enough."

### Gate 1: Credibility
**Threshold: 0.75**

Source track record, corroboration status, and primary vs. secondary distance are evaluated. We weight independent primary sources higher than secondary reporting. We downweight sources with documented history of inaccuracy or ideological distortion.

### Gate 2: Volume
**Minimum: 3 Independent Sources**

A signal must appear in independent mentions across unrelated sources. We measure source independence, not repost count or amplification. One source republished across 100 platforms counts as one source.

### Gate 3: Velocity
**Window: 72 Hours**

We measure the rate of independent corroboration over a fixed window. Rapid corroboration from unrelated sources suggests factual substance. Slow adoption or concentrated amplification suggests narrative construction.

### Gate 4: Novelty
**Check: Deduplication Against Prior Analysis**

The signal must be new. We flag candidate signals already analyzed in prior briefs and exclude them from independent counting. We do not republish yesterday's framing as today's news.

### What Happens After the Gates

A signal that passes all four gates moves to human editorial judgment. An editor receives the full evidence chain: every source, every timestamp, every corroboration. The editor's job is synthesis and framing—connecting the signal to context, identifying what it means, and suggesting where it leads.

No algorithm writes what you read. No confidence score ships without human review.

## Confidence Language

We use four terms, always with a numeric score attached:

**CONFIRMED** (0.85+)  
Multiple independent primary sources, direct documentation, or official acknowledgment. No reasonable dispute on fact. Analytical disagreement remains possible; factual certainty does not.

**HIGH CONFIDENCE** (0.70–0.84)  
Multiple independent secondary sources or credible primary reporting. Strong corroboration. Documented evidence. Reasonable confidence for operational planning or policy response.

**MEDIUM CONFIDENCE** (0.50–0.69)  
Limited independent sources, partial corroboration, or inference from public record. Actionable but contingent. Suitable for hypothesis generation or further investigation.

**LOW CONFIDENCE** (0.25–0.49)  
Single source, weak corroboration, significant inference required, or speculative pattern. Not actionable for policy or strategy. Useful for tracking emerging signals; insufficient for publication as fact.

Scores below 0.25 do not pass the gates and do not appear in briefs.

## Where Our Method Fails

### Coordinated Disinformation
The gates measure independence of sources, not truth. A well-coordinated disinformation campaign with genuinely independent-looking amplification vectors can pass Volume and Velocity. We cannot distinguish between authentic distributed truth and authentic-looking distributed fiction.

**Mitigation:** Heavy weighting on primary sources and official documentary evidence. Regular backtesting against later-revealed facts. Confidence scores reflect this uncertainty explicitly.

### Access to Classified or Private Information
We only see what's public. Closed-door negotiations, classified assessments, private communications, and trade secrets are outside our reach by design and by law. If a brief says "unconfirmed," that may reflect classification barriers, not evidentiary weakness.

**Mitigation:** Transparent statement of what we don't know. Explicit flagging of inference from public signals to private intent.

### Editorial Framing Bias
The gates are deterministic; the sentence-level framing is not. Editors write the narrative. We publish our source list specifically so you don't have to trust our framing—check the sources directly.

**Mitigation:** Complete source register. Linkable evidence chains. Separation of fact from analysis in every published brief.

## What We Publish and Why

Notitia Civica publishes its own limitations on the record because a methodology you can't interrogate isn't a methodology—it's a claim to authority.

We believe:

1. **You should see the gate a claim passed through.** Not trust the verdict; see the evidence.
2. **You should know the confidence score it earned.** And understand exactly what that score means.
3. **You should be able to audit the source trail.** Every fact we publish includes its source register.

This is harder than publishing unvetted rumor or unexamined consensus. It is also more useful.
