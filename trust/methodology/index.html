Methodology

Software finds leads and helps write drafts. A named human editor decides what is published.

Sealing sources

Each source is saved exactly as retrieved, and its SHA-256 digest is recorded in an append-only ledger, which refuses updates and deletions of sealed records. The source ledger on every article lists those digests, so a sealed copy can be checked against them.

Source tiers

Tier 1 primary
The original record, such as enrolled bill text or a regulator's filing.
Tier 2 institutional
Material published by an established institution.
Tier 3 secondary
Reporting or analysis that describes a primary record.
Tier 4 unverified
Material that has not yet been checked against a stronger source.

Finding leads

Rule-based software scores public records for unusual signals. Each lead must pass four deterministic checks: deduplication, authority, corroboration, and freshness and anomaly.

Drafting

Drafts are written with Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. It sees only the sealed sources for that lead and must cite them. A local AI model may add notes for editors, but those notes never decide whether a lead moves forward and are never published as findings.

Automated gates

Five checks must pass before a draft can be signed:

  1. Headlines cannot be questions, teasers, or clickbait.
  2. Loaded language and anonymous attributions such as "critics say" are flagged.
  3. Every paragraph must cite a real source, and every direct quote must appear word for word in a source that paragraph cites.
  4. Personal data is flagged, and allegations against private people are blocked until a right of reply is logged.
  5. No single type of source can exceed 60% of citations. The exception is a fact brief that rests on one Tier 1 primary document.

Human sign-off

An editor confirms four things: they read every cited primary source, checked every quote, reviewed the piece for fairness, and reviewed privacy and defamation risk. The system then records a SHA-256 proof of the text, its sources, the editor's name, and the time of signing.

Checking a proof

The proof hash is the SHA-256 of these lines joined by newlines: the article text, the sorted source IDs one per line, the editor's name, and the UTC signing time. Any change to those inputs produces a different hash.

Retractions

A retracted article stays online under a permanent notice that explains why. The ledger refuses edits to that notice. The proof hash still covers the text as signed, so it shows what we published, not that it stands.

What this does not prove

A proof shows what an editor signed and when. It does not show that an article is correct. Automated gates catch specific patterns, not every error, which is why uncertainties are published and corrections are public.
