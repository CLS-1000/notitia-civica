Brownsville Border Crossing Sees Estimated Wait Times of Up to 80 Minutes for Passenger Vehicles

A U.S. Customs and Border Protection feed posted a 75-minute gap between passenger and commercial vehicle wait times at the Veterans International crossing in Brownsville on Nov. 11, 2026.

U.S. Customs and Border Protection posted an estimated 80-minute wait for standard passenger vehicles alongside a 5-minute wait for standard commercial vehicles at the Brownsville Veterans International port of entry (port 535502) at 6:00 p.m. CST on Nov. 11, 2026 [S-1000082]S-1000082Tier 1 primary
bwt.cbp.gov.

According to the agency's port feed, one of eight maximum passenger vehicle lanes was open for standard manual processing with an 80-minute delay, two Ready Lanes were open with an 80-minute delay, and two NEXUS/SENTRI lanes were open with a 30-minute delay [S-1000082]S-1000082Tier 1 primary
bwt.cbp.gov.

On the freight side, two of four maximum commercial vehicle lanes were open under RFID processing with a 5-minute wait and no reported delay, while FAST lanes were listed as closed [S-1000082]S-1000082Tier 1 primary
bwt.cbp.gov.

Standard pedestrian processing had one of two maximum lanes open with zero minutes of delay, and Ready Lanes for pedestrians were listed as not applicable; the crossing operates from 6 a.m. to midnight and reported open status with no construction notice [S-1000082]S-1000082Tier 1 primary
bwt.cbp.gov.

Verified

  • Port 535502 (Brownsville, Veterans International) reported a 5-minute wait for standard commercial lanes (2 of 4 lanes open, RFID) at 6:00 p.m. CST on 11/11/2026.
  • Standard passenger lanes (1 of 8 lanes open, Manual) and passenger Ready Lanes (2 lanes open) each reported an 80-minute delay at 6:00 p.m. CST on 11/11/2026, while NEXUS/SENTRI lanes (2 open) reported a 30-minute delay.
  • Commercial FAST lanes were listed as closed, and standard pedestrian processing (1 of 2 lanes open) reported a 0-minute wait.

Uncertain

  • U.S. Customs and Border Protection posted wait times are point-in-time estimates; the sealed source does not show whether delays persisted across later updates.
  • The port feed lists open lane counts and closed FAST lanes but does not explain staffing levels or why additional passenger lanes were closed.
  • The sealed source contains no information on commercial cargo volumes, specific commuter delays, or downstream traffic impacts.
