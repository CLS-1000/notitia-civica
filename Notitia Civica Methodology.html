Methodology

How We Work

Four deterministic gates decide what a human ever sees. No model writes what you read. No claim ships without a confidence score. Here is exactly how, and where it can fail.

The Gate Architecture

Every candidate signal — a document, a post, a filing — passes through four gates in sequence. All four must pass. There is no override, no editorial exception, no "close enough."

A signal that fails any gate is discarded, not queued, not flagged for review. It does not reach an editor. This is by design: the gates exist to keep human judgment scarce and applied only where it earns its cost.

What Happens After the Gates

A signal that passes all four gates is handed to a human editor with its full evidence chain: every source, every timestamp, every independent corroboration. The editor's job is synthesis and verification, not discovery — the gates already did discovery. The editor decides confidence language, writes the narrative, and is the only point where judgment enters the pipeline.

Confidence Language

We use four terms, always with a numeric score attached: CONFIRMED (0.85+, multiple independent primary sources), LIKELY (0.65–0.84, strong corroboration with some inference), POSSIBLE (0.4–0.64, plausible but thin), HOLD (below 0.4, insufficient — not published as fact, only as an open question if published at all).

Where This Fails

The gates measure independence of sources, not truth. A well-coordinated disinformation campaign with genuinely independent-looking amplification vectors can pass Volume and Velocity. We catch this most of the time through the Credibility gate's track-record weighting — not always.

We only see what's public. Closed-door negotiations, classified assessments, and private communications are outside our reach by design and by law. If a brief says "unconfirmed," that may mean the truth is simply not visible to open sources yet — not that nothing is happening.

Editors write the narrative. The gates are deterministic; the sentence-level framing is not. We publish our source list specifically so you don't have to trust our framing — check the sources yourself.

Why We Publish This

Every other intelligence product asks you to trust the verdict. We think that's backwards. You should be able to see the gate a claim passed through, the confidence score it earned, and the exact sources behind it — and decide for yourself whether our judgment matches your own reading of the evidence. If our methodology looks wrong to you, tell us. We'll publish the correction with the same rigor as the original claim.
